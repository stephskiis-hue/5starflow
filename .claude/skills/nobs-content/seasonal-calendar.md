# Winnipeg yard calendar (research base for content)

Starting facts for each month, researched Oct 5 2026. Winnipeg is zone 3, heavy Red River clay, frost-free season roughly May 21 to Sept 15 to 20. Upper Midwest extension advice (University of Minnesota, NDSU) fits our climate better than Ontario or US-south sites.

**How to use this file:** pick the month, pick a topic, then **re-check the source that night** (open the URL, confirm the number still says what is written here) and look for one more angle. Put every URL you used in `grounding`. If a source changed, fix this file in the same run. Never quote a number from a commercial lawn blog when an extension service or the City says something different. Example: several Winnipeg snow companies' blogs say homeowners must clear city sidewalks, but the City's own policy says it plows residential sidewalks itself.

Primary sources, best first: City of Winnipeg (winnipeg.ca, legacy.winnipeg.ca), Province of Manitoba (gov.mb.ca, manitoba.ca), Trees Winnipeg (treeswinnipeg.org), University of Minnesota Extension (extension.umn.edu, turf.umn.edu), NDSU Extension (ndsu.edu/agriculture/extension), Environment Canada (weather.gc.ca), then our own site and real jobs.

## January to February (off season: snow)
- **City sidewalks.** The City plows residential (Priority 3) sidewalks to a packed snow surface, usually after an inspection following about 5 cm. Residential streets usually after about 10 cm, finished within about 36 hours of starting. Driveway windrows over 20 cm are normally removed from approaches. Source: https://legacy.winnipeg.ca/publicworks/snow/snow-clearing-policy.stm
  Angle: "The City does your sidewalk. Your driveway and walk are on you." Then the snow plan (monthly contracts only, nobs-brand).
- Ice dams and salt on new sod or patios (research before posting; cite the source).

## March
- **Spring cankerworm bands** must be on trees by March 15 (or keep last fall's bands clean). Source: https://legacy.winnipeg.ca/publicworks/insectcontrol/cankerworms/treebanding.stm
- **Dormant seeding** still works while soil is below about 40°F (4°C), before green up. Source: https://turf.umn.edu/now-perfect-time-dormant-seed-lawns-minnesota
- Book spring cleanup now (OfferPost, no price).

## April
- **Elm pruning ban April 1 to July 31** (Dutch elm disease; fresh cuts attract elm bark beetles). Storing elm firewood with bark is illegal in Manitoba. Source: https://www.gov.mb.ca/stopthespread/fis/ded/faq.html and https://www.treeswinnipeg.org/our-urban-forest/urban-forest-threats/dutch-elm-disease
- **Don't rake too early.** Wait until soil dries and mud season passes. Rake matted grass to break up snow mold. Source: https://extension.umn.edu/news/practice-patience-spring-lawn-care
- Snow mold: what the grey or pink matted patches are, and why they usually recover. Source: https://extension.umn.edu/yard-and-garden-news/what-does-winter-injury-look-your-lawn

## May
- **Yard waste collection starts** the week of May 4 (Area A) and May 11 (Area B), every two weeks on recycling day. Brown paper yard bags, cardboard boxes or a lidless container, max 22 kg. Branches bundled, max 10 cm thick and 1 m long. Source: https://www.winnipeg.ca/yardwaste
- **Crabgrass preventer** when the top 1 to 2 inches of soil sits above 55°F (13°C) for a few days (for us usually mid to late May). Source: https://extension.umn.edu/weeds/crabgrass
- **Last frost** averages about May 21 to 25. Hold tender plantings. Source: https://www.almanac.com/gardening/frostdates/MB/Winnipeg (cross-check with Environment Canada).
- **First mow:** the one-third rule. To keep 3 inches, cut at about 4.5. Source: https://extension.umn.edu/news/practice-patience-spring-lawn-care
- Pesticides: since Nov 2022 Manitoba lets homeowners use Health Canada approved products on their own lawns, but not in "sensitive areas" (schools, hospitals, child care, playgrounds, dog parks, provincial parks). Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/manitoba/manitoba-amends-cosmetic-pesticide-legislation-associations-1.6384601 (confirm on manitoba.ca before posting).
- **Remove cankerworm bands by early June** or the bark can rot. Source: tree banding link above.

## June to July
- Mowing height in heat, watering deeply and less often, grubs, dog spots. Research each one fresh (extension.umn.edu lawn care pages).
- **Emerald ash borer.** Confirmed in Winnipeg since 2017. The City treats only public ash trees; ash on private property is the owner's job, injected by a licensed applicator every 1 to 2 years. Suspect a tree? Call 311. Source: https://legacy.winnipeg.ca/publicworks/parksopenspace/urbanforestry/EmeraldAsh.stm
- Elm pruning ban still on until July 31.

## August
- **Aeration and overseeding window opens:** late August through September for Winnipeg. Core aeration only on Red River clay (spike aeration compacts clay more). Plugs break down in 2 to 4 weeks. Cool season grass needs about 6 weeks before freeze up to establish from seed. Sources: https://lawnnorder.ca/lawn-aeration-guide-winnipeg/ and https://growersguide.ca/blog/best-time-aerate-overseed-lawn-canada (industry sites; back them with an extension page such as https://extension.umd.edu/resource/lawn-aeration).
- **Fall feeding season starts:** UMN's nitrogen table puts fall feedings in August, September and early October (fewer, September only, for low input lawns). Add phosphorus only when a soil test says so; many midwest and prairie soils are already high. Source: https://extension.umn.edu/lawncare/fertilizing-lawns
  Not yet verified (find a primary source before using): "two thirds of the year's nitrogen in fall", specific grades like 24-0-12.

## September
- **Fall fertilizer** (see August). "Winterizer" products are mostly urea nitrogen plus potassium; the nitrogen does the work and results vary. Phosphorus rarely needs adding. Sources: https://extension.umn.edu/about/our-stories/news/late-fall-lawn-care and https://extension.umn.edu/lawncare/fertilizing-lawns
- **Fall cankerworm bands** on by the end of September or before the first hard frost; leave them on all winter. Source: https://legacy.winnipeg.ca/publicworks/insectcontrol/cankerworms/treebanding.stm
- **First frost** averages about Sept 15 to 20.
- Fall is the best time for broadleaf weed control (dandelions pull food down to the roots). Research and cite before posting.
- Water evergreens a little less in September so they harden off, then soak them in October (see October).

## October
- **Last mows:** cut a little shorter going into winter, but not lower than 2 inches (about 2 to 2.5 over the last few cuts). Source: https://extension.umn.edu/about/our-stories/news/late-fall-lawn-care
- **Snow mold in three steps:** shorter final cut, no heavy late nitrogen (skip fall feeding if snow mold keeps coming back), deal with leaf litter. Source: https://extension.umn.edu/news/fall-lawn-care-3 (the page blocks bots; open it in Chrome to confirm). A figure of "80% less damage" circulates for these steps but was not confirmed on the page; don't use it until you see it there.
- **Leaves:** mulch light cover with the mower, remove heavy cover (it blocks light and traps moisture). Compost or use in beds. Yard waste goes in brown paper bags (not plastic). Sources: UMN late fall link above and https://www.winnipeg.ca/yardwaste
- **Irrigation blowout** before the first hard freeze. Source: UMN late fall link above.
- **Evergreens (cedars):** deep soak until freeze up. Burlap screen on the south, southwest and windward sides, top open; never wrap tight. 3 to 4 inches of bark mulch at the base. Anti-desiccant sprays don't work. Source: https://extension.umn.edu/planting-and-growing-guides/protecting-trees-and-shrubs-winter
- **Snow plan sign ups** (ComparePost, monthly contracts only).

## November
- **Dormant seeding** once soil has been below about 45°F (7°C) for several days and before steady snow cover, usually early to mid November. Freeze and thaw works the seed into the soil; it sprouts in spring. Source: https://turf.umn.edu/now-perfect-time-dormant-seed-lawns-minnesota
- Rabbit and vole guards on young trees and shrubs (research and cite).
- Snow season starts: what the City clears vs what you clear (see January).

## December
- Snow plan reminders, salt and de-icer damage to lawns and concrete (research), year in review (real jobs, real reviews).

### Turning a fact into a post that is worth reading
- Lead with the Winnipeg specific: a date, a number, a local rule.
- Give the **why** (one line), the **how** (a number), and the **mistake people make**.
- One fact per graphic; the caption carries the detail in 3 to 6 short lines; a carousel (3 to 6 slides) for a full how-to ("Fall lawn plan: aerate, feed, last mow, leaves, blowout").
- Never invent a stat. Rounded numbers from the source are fine; changed numbers are not.
