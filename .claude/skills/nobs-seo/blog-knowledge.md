# No-BS Yardwork blog knowledge base (writer guide)

Read by the blog writer (`nobs-seo`) every run. Taken from the live site's `_blog-knowledge.md` on 2026-10-21 and trimmed for the server-side publisher.

**What changed:** the 5StarFlow server now builds the page (head tags, JSON-LD, navigation, footer, hero image, byline and the closing quote box) and uploads it. You send only the title, meta description, h1, keyword, hero photo and the **body HTML**. So the old template block (section A) and the Pexels recipes (section H) are gone, and nothing below about uploading, `posts.json`, `blog.html` or sitemaps applies to you.

**Body HTML rules (the server checks all of them before anything is uploaded):**
- Allowed tags: `p, h2, h3, h4, ul, ol, li, strong, em, a, br, blockquote, table`, plus `div` with class `blog-inline-cta` or `blog-callout`. No H1, no images, no scripts, no `style` attributes, no comments.
- The two CTAs inside the body are `<div class="blog-inline-cta"><p>... <a href="...">...</a></p></div>`. The end quote box (CTA #3 below) is added by the server: do not write it.
- Internal links: write them as `https://www.no-bs-yardwork.com/<page>` (the server also accepts `contact.html`-style links and rewrites them). Every link must go to a live page, and every `required` link in your brief must appear.
- Title 60 characters or fewer with the keyword in it, meta description 155 or fewer, the keyword in the h1 and in the first 100 words, one answer in the first two sentences.
- No banned words, no mid-sentence dashes, only the phone number 204-900-0438, no claims like "guarantee" or "best in Winnipeg". Never quote a No-Bs price; typical Winnipeg market ranges in a cost guide are fine when labelled and dated.

## B. Title formulas (rotate; one per post)

Must match exactly one of these — the whole point is to mirror real search queries.

1. **Cost** — `How Much Does {Service} Cost in Winnipeg? (2026 Pricing Guide)`
2. **Timing** — `When Should You {Verb} Your Lawn in Winnipeg?` / `When Is the Best Time to {X} in Winnipeg?`
3. **Need** — `Do You Need {Service} in Winnipeg?` / `Signs Your Winnipeg Lawn Needs {X}`
4. **Comparison** — `{A} vs {B}: Which Does Your Winnipeg Lawn Need?`
5. **Frequency** — `How Often Should You {X} in Winnipeg?`
6. **Tips** — `N {Adjective} {Service} Tips That Actually Work in Winnipeg`

Rules:
- Include "Winnipeg" in the title.
- Years use the current calendar year.
- Keep titles ≤70 chars where possible.

## E. Hook patterns (2-line direct answer)

Rule: sentence 1 = the answer. Sentence 2 = what the post will cover.

**Cost:**
- "Sod in Winnipeg usually runs $0.80–$1.50/sq ft installed, depending on grading, prep, and access. Here's what actually moves the price and when sod beats seeding."

**Timing:**
- "The best time to aerate your lawn in Winnipeg is late April through mid-May, or again in early September. Here's how to know which window your yard needs."

**Need:**
- "If your Winnipeg lawn feels spongy, grew thin last summer, or has a thatch layer thicker than half an inch, you need power raking this spring. Here's how to tell for sure."

**Comparison:**
- "Power raking pulls up dead thatch. Aeration punches cores to relieve compaction. Most Winnipeg lawns need both — but in a specific order. Here's which one yours needs first."

**Frequency:**
- "In Winnipeg, grass should be cut every 5–7 days from late May through August — longer between cuts stresses the lawn. Here's the schedule that keeps your yard healthy."
